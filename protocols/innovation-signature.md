# Innovation Signature Protocol

## Purpose

Normalize a paper or candidate into a compact mechanism signature so that comparison is about a claimed causal move, not topic words, module names, or conference outcomes.

## Signature Shape

Every signature records five linked fields:

1. `bottleneck`: the specific failure, limitation, or unresolved dependency;
2. `operation`: the proposed intervention or explanatory operation;
3. `changed_object`: the representation, objective, assignment, constraint, inference object, or other object actually changed;
4. `critical_condition`: the scope in which the move is claimed to matter;
5. `predicted_contrast`: an observable difference from a credible baseline or alternative.

Add scope, subject/version, epistemic status, linked claims, and linked evidence. A paper signature is `FACT` only where a directly verified author statement supports it; use `INFERENCE` for normalized interpretation. A candidate signature is a `HYPOTHESIS`, never an observed fact.

## Creation

- In S04, create one evidence-linked signature for every comparison-critical paper family at R2 or deeper.
- In S06, create a proposed signature for every candidate and attach it to a draft core commitment.
- In S10, supersede rather than overwrite a candidate signature after innovation peeling.
- In S09, compare a frozen candidate signature against each closest competitor dimension by dimension.

Do not use a signature as a score, a taxonomy label, or a replacement for reading the source. It is an index into evidence and a prompt for adversarial comparison.

## Specificity Gate

A candidate cannot advance as a novelty survivor if its signature lacks a meaningful bottleneck, changed object, critical condition, or predicted contrast. The correct outcome is `REFINE` or `KILL`, not a more decorative title.

For each candidate, ask:

- Would two technically distinct methods receive different signatures?
- Can a skeptical reviewer state the competing signature without relying on its paper name?
- Does the predicted contrast distinguish the mechanism from a generic improvement claim?

If not, create reasoning debt `RD-` for vague mechanism/novelty and reopen S06 or S10 as appropriate.

## Collision Mapping

For a candidate and competitor, compare all five fields using the existing overlap cells: `NONE`, `PARTIAL`, `HIGH`, `SUBSUMES`.

- A matching name or task alone is not a signature collision.
- A same bottleneck plus operation that changes the same object under the same or broader condition is a strong collision candidate.
- A different implementation may still be subsumed if the claimed mechanism and predicted contrast are already established.
- A different condition or contrast can be a residual only when it is evidence-linked, scientifically material, and produces a distinct falsifiable prediction.

Record the mapping in the threat evidence; do not infer a formal T4/T5 until the reading-depth rules in `protocols/reading.md` and `protocols/novelty.md` are met.

## Outcome-Data Boundary

Do not rank, generate, or rescue candidates with acceptance rates, citation rates, pattern frequencies, or outcome-derived priors. Historical pattern cards may be used only as optional vocabulary for generating alternatives after their source cards and limitations are recorded. Bottleneck fit, evidence, falsifiability, and threat analysis determine advancement.
