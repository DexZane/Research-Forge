# Literature Triage and Access Protocol

## Purpose

Choose what to read next under time and access constraints without mistaking a priority score for scientific truth.

## Triage Queue

Create a `LT-` record for each decision-relevant paper family or source route. Record decision question, target record IDs, required reading tier, threat/coverage role, full-text/supplement/code access status, expected information gain, deadline, blocking state, and next action.

Prioritize lexicographically:

1. blocking integrity, emergency, formal T4/T5, and gate-critical unknowns;
2. expected ability to change a decision, boundary, or falsifier;
3. highest uncertainty on a core mechanism or competitor;
4. access-ready sources before inaccessible sources of equal decision value;
5. enrichment last.

Use qualitative `HIGH`/`MEDIUM`/`LOW` expected information gain. Never fabricate numerical probabilities or treat an inaccessible source as irrelevant.

## Access States

Use `FULL_TEXT_READY`, `ABSTRACT_ONLY`, `SUPPLEMENT_MISSING`, `CODE_MISSING`, `ACCESS_REQUESTED`, `UNAVAILABLE`, or `NOT_APPLICABLE`.

- Only `FULL_TEXT_READY` closes an R2/R3/R4 reading requirement. `ABSTRACT_ONLY`, `ACCESS_REQUESTED`, `UNAVAILABLE`, `SUPPLEMENT_MISSING`, and `CODE_MISSING` remain open for deep-reading closure.
- An unavailable decision-critical source creates `MISSING_FULL_TEXT` debt and caps the associated judgment.
- Record legal/authorized access routes only. Do not bypass paywalls or infer unobserved methods from citations.

## Reading Budget and Exit

Set a time/source budget by decision, not a paper-count target. Before G2/G3/G4, list the highest-priority unresolved queue items and whether they block the gate. A queue is complete when all gate-critical items are resolved, explicitly held, or bounded as inaccessible—not when every discovered paper has been read.
